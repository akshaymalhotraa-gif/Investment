# National Grid — Strategic Questions for Management Meeting

**Prepared: 9 July 2026** | Meeting: week of 13 July 2026 (AGM is 14 July)
**Companion note:** `national-grid-guidance-outlook.md` (guidance history and sources)

The setup: management has just upgraded the framework — ≥£70bn capex to FY31, 8–10% underlying EPS CAGR, "no new ordinary equity" — but the stock fell ~7% on results day. The market's doubt is not about the growth; it's about the *quality and funding* of the growth. These questions are built to test exactly that. Each one has a "listen for" note: the tell that separates a confident answer from a rehearsed one.

---

## The one question, if you only get one

> **"You're investing £70bn at an Ofgem-allowed real return of 6.12% and guiding group RoE of ~9%. Walk me through why a marginal pound of this capex creates shareholder value rather than just asset growth — and what would have to happen for you to conclude it doesn't?"**

This is the entire investment case in one question. The plan only works if returns genuinely exceed the cost of capital *after* the scrip dilution and rising leverage. **Listen for:** whether they can decompose the ~9% RoE into base allowed return vs. incentives vs. financing outperformance, and whether they treat "asset growth" and "value creation" as the same thing. If they conflate the two, that's the red flag.

---

## A. Capital allocation & returns — is growth actually value?

**1. "The market took 7% off the stock the day you announced the upgraded framework. What do you think investors are getting wrong — and what might they be getting right?"**
A deliberately open, hard-hitting opener. Good management teams answer the second half honestly (gearing trajectory, scrip dilution, execution risk). Evasive ones only answer the first half. **Listen for:** genuine acknowledgment of the funding concern vs. "the market misunderstood us."

**2. "Of the 8–10% EPS CAGR, how much is base regulated return, how much is incentive/operational outperformance you have to earn every year, and how much is financial engineering — scrip assumptions, hybrids, semi-nominal returns?"**
The EPS upgrade (from 6–8%) landed simultaneously with gearing guidance drifting to high-60s%. You want to know how much of the growth is *bought with leverage*. **Listen for:** a clean split. If they can't or won't give one, the quality of the CAGR is lower than the headline.

**3. "You've said returns should be 'internationally comparable.' If a US utility offered you the same capex at a 9.5–10% allowed nominal ROE with lower political risk, why is the marginal pound still going to UK transmission?"**
Tests whether capital allocation is a real discipline or a regulatory obligation dressed up as strategy. **Listen for:** whether they admit the UK spend is largely non-discretionary (ASTI/licence obligations) — an honest answer — versus pretending it's a free choice.

**4. "What is the hurdle rate below which you would hand capex back — refuse a project, defer it, or push it to a later price control? Has that ever actually happened?"**
A capital allocator has a walk-away price. **Listen for:** a concrete example of a project declined or deferred. "We've never had to" means the discipline is untested.

## B. Balance sheet & funding — the credibility question

**5. "In 2023 the message was also 'no new equity' — then came the £7bn rights issue in 2024. What is structurally different this time that makes the no-new-equity commitment more durable, given gearing is now guided *back up* to the high-60s%?"**
This is the hardest fair question you can ask, and the one they will have rehearsed least comfortably. The 2024 rights issue surprised the market after similar assurances. **Listen for:** specifics — headroom to rating thresholds in basis points, hybrid capacity in £bn, sensitivity to a downside scenario — not adjectives like "strong" and "comfortable."

**6. "If something breaks — a rating threat, a cost shock on ASTI, a bad rate case — what gives first: the capex plan, the dividend, or the no-equity commitment? Rank them."**
Forces a priority ordering they usually avoid stating. **Listen for:** whether the dividend is genuinely sacrosanct or quietly third in line. The ranking tells you what the real risk to your income stream is.

**7. "The 25% scrip uptake assumption is doing quiet work in your framework — it's equity issuance by another name, roughly £300m+ a year of dilution. At what share price does scrip stop making sense, and would you buy back the dilution if the balance sheet allowed?"**
Non-pedantic way to expose that "no new equity" has an asterisk. **Listen for:** whether they acknowledge scrip is dilutive equity, and whether there's any scenario where per-share thinking beats balance-sheet convenience.

**8. "Interest rates staying higher for longer: at what gilt yield does this plan stop working — either because your real cost of debt outruns allowances or because income investors can get 4.5% risk-free?"**
Semi-nominal returns in RIIO-T3 changed the inflation/rates exposure. **Listen for:** whether they've genuinely stress-tested the framework against rates, and how well they can explain the (complex) inflation linkage simply. If they can't explain it simply, the market never will either — a permanent valuation drag.

## C. Execution — the £31bn UK delivery machine

**9. "You're increasing UK Electricity Transmission spend by 150% in five years. Name the single scarcest input — HVDC cable, transformers, skilled labour, consents — and tell me what you've locked versus what you're still exposed on."**
Everyone says "supply chain is secured for three-quarters of the plan." Make them name the residual quarter. **Listen for:** one specific bottleneck named with a specific mitigation. Vague answers ("we have strong supplier relationships") mean the exposure is real.

**10. "In RIIO-T3, if a major ASTI project runs two years late and 30% over budget, who eats it — you or the consumer? Where exactly does the risk-sharing stop protecting you?"**
The asymmetry of the new deal in one question. Delay penalties and totex sharing mean execution failure hits shareholders. **Listen for:** an honest description of the sharing factors and delivery incentives, and whether they'll quantify their downside exposure on a single flagship project.

**11. "What did you get wrong operationally in RIIO-T2, and what specifically have you changed for T3 other than 'lessons learned'?"**
Backward-looking honesty predicts forward-looking credibility. **Listen for:** a real named failure (a project, a forecast, a consenting delay), not a humblebrag.

## D. UK political & regulatory risk — the five-year bet

**12. "Network charges are rising visibly on consumer bills just as affordability politics intensify. What stops a future government or Ofgem reopening the deal mid-period — and what protection do you actually have if they do?"**
RIIO-T3 is accepted, but five years is long, and an election falls inside it. The Spanish and other precedents show regulated 'settlements' can be revisited under political pressure. **Listen for:** legal/licence protections named specifically, plus evidence they're managing the politics (how they talk about consumer bills), not just the regulation.

**13. "Zonal pricing and wider market reform could reshape where and how much transmission is needed. If REMA-style reform lands, does any part of the £31bn become a stranded-asset question?"**
Big-picture, tests whether the capex is robust to market redesign rather than assuming today's build-out map is final. **Listen for:** whether they've mapped projects to reform scenarios or dismiss the question.

## E. The US — the other half of the story

**14. "The FERC order set base ROE at 9.57% and you're petitioning for 11.39%. Beyond that dispute — what's your realistic view of achieved US returns over the next five years, and is New York becoming politically harder to earn in than the UK?"**
~£29bn of the plan is US. NY affordability politics (and downstate rate-case cycles) are a live risk. **Listen for:** whether they treat the US as diversification or as a second political-risk exposure; how candid they are about the gap between allowed and achieved ROE.

**15. "Data-centre and electrification load growth is turbocharging US peer valuations. How much genuine load-growth upside is in New York and New England — and is any of it in your numbers, or is it all option value?"**
The upside question — you're probing for what's *not* in guidance. **Listen for:** specific interconnection-queue or large-load numbers. This is where the bull case gets better than the sell-side models.

## F. Portfolio & endgame

**16. "You've sold ESO, gas transmission, Grain LNG, and renewables — the pure-play pivot is done. What does National Grid look like in 2035, and is a UK/US split or a sale of one side ever on the whiteboard?"**
Tests whether there's a strategic endgame beyond executing the plan, and whether the conglomerate discount between the UK and US businesses is something they think about. **Listen for:** whether the answer is a vision or a five-year plan restated.

**17. "What's the most likely reason this plan fails? Not the tail risk — the base-rate reason."**
The classic closer. Every strong management team has a real answer (usually execution/supply chain or politics). **Listen for:** speed and specificity. Hesitation here, after 16 questions of confidence, tells you the risk register is thinner than the investor deck.

## If time is short — the five that matter

1. The one question (marginal pound: value vs. asset growth) — *the investment case*
2. Q5 (why is "no new equity" credible this time?) — *the funding risk*
3. Q6 (what gives first: capex, dividend, or no-equity?) — *the dividend risk*
4. Q9 (name the scarcest input on the £31bn build) — *the execution risk*
5. Q12 (what stops the deal being reopened politically?) — *the regime risk*

## How to read the meeting overall

- **Bullish tells:** they volunteer the bear case unprompted; they decompose returns cleanly; they name specific bottlenecks and specific mitigations; they talk per-share, not just asset growth.
- **Bearish tells:** "strong and resilient" adjectives instead of numbers; treating the 7% results-day fall as market ignorance; inability to rank capex vs. dividend vs. balance sheet; no example of ever refusing capex.
