# Profitable, Ex-Healthcare Growth Themes — Where Earnings Are Inflecting but the Equity Hasn't Re-Rated

**Compiled: 9 July 2026**

**Revised mandate (tightened from the earlier note):**
- **No negative earnings** — every name below is GAAP-profitable *today*, generating real net income and free cash flow. This deletes the earlier Tier-1 diagnostics/GLP-1 ideas (mostly unprofitable) — correct call.
- **Ex-healthcare** entirely.
- Still excluded: **data-center/hyperscaler, semiconductors, aerospace/defense.**
- **Already-known / expensive names are not the answer.** We've covered subsea cable and Quanta; Quanta is a fully-discovered, ~high-20s-P/E name. The point here is themes where the *fundamentals have already moved* (1–3 quarters of hard numbers) but the *multiple hasn't*.

**The through-line:** all three themes below share one setup — **the P&L has inflected, management is putting cash where its mouth is (record returns / raised targets), and the equity is lagging the fundamental.** That gap is the opportunity.

## Theme 1 — Precious-metals miners: the operating-leverage + capital-return re-rating *(highest conviction)*

**The one-line thesis:** gold and silver are at records, but miner *costs* are roughly fixed — so every incremental dollar of metal price drops almost straight to free cash flow. Margins are expanding *faster* than the metal, the companies are now debt-free and returning half their FCF, **yet the equities have badly lagged the commodity.** This is the cleanest "earnings already inflected, multiple hasn't" setup in the market, and generalists are structurally under-owned.

### The numbers already printed (Q1 2026 — this is the last quarter, not a forecast)
**Gold**
- **Kinross (KGC):** attributable FCF **more than doubled to $837.5m** (from $380.8m a year ago) — the **4th consecutive record-FCF quarter**. Margin per oz **+92% to a record $3,476**, outpacing the realized gold price by 20%+. Returned **>$1bn since April 2025**; buybacks + dividend running.
- **Agnico Eagle (AEM):** net income **$1.70bn ($3.39/sh), +109% YoY**; FCF **$732m**; **net cash $2.9bn**; returned **~$375m (~50% of FCF)** in the quarter; **dividend raised 12.5%**; FY guidance confirmed.

**Silver** (higher operating leverage — a smaller price move swings margins more)
- **Pan American (PAAS):** Q1 FCF **$488m**; record **$1.8bn** cash; new framework targeting **up to $1bn of returns in 2026** (buybacks + a meaningful dividend increase).
- **Hecla (HL):** **record** adjusted EBITDA **$265m** and record FCF **$144m**; **entered Q2 debt-free**; projecting **>$700m FCF for 2026**.

### Management is explicitly leaning in
Every one of these lifted its shareholder-return framework this quarter — dividend hikes, enlarged buybacks, "record cash." That is management telling you the cash flow is durable, not a one-quarter spike.

### Why it's *not* priced (the actual edge)
Despite record fundamentals, **the equities sold off into a spot-silver pullback** — a textbook fundamentals-vs-price disconnect. The miners have lagged the metal for the better part of two years; the sector is under-owned by generalist funds and still carries the "value-trap" reputation from the 2013–2019 capital-destruction era. What's different now: balance sheets are net-cash, capex discipline is real, and FCF yields are high even at spot (Hecla alone guiding >$700m FCF). You're buying record cash generation at a de-rated multiple.

### How to play it
- **Lower-beta core:** Agnico (best-in-class, net cash, growing dividend) and Kinross (FCF momentum, cheap).
- **Higher operating leverage:** Pan American and Hecla (silver mix amplifies margin on any further price move) — more torque, more volatility.

### Risks / watch items
The whole thesis rides on the metal price holding — miners are a *leveraged* bet on gold/silver, so a sustained commodity drawdown hits them harder than the metal; jurisdiction risk (some assets in higher-risk geographies); and the sector's own history of blowing FCF windfalls on bad M&A — so **capital-return discipline is the thing to monitor every quarter.**

## Theme 2 — Independent ("boutique") M&A advisers: the deal-cycle upswing *(financials, asset-light)*

**The one-line thesis:** the M&A cycle turned in 2025 and is *early-to-mid*, not late. The independent advisers are asset-light, ~mid-teens-to-20%+ revenue growers right now, pay out most of their earnings, and trade below where they peaked last cycle despite backlogs that are already "locked in." Profitable, high-ROE, and levered to a multi-year normalization — not a hyperscaler capex bet.

### The numbers (last 1–2 quarters + FY25 exit rate)
- **Global M&A hit ~$4.5tn in 2025, +49% YoY**; **Q1 2026 industry IB fees +28% YoY**; megadeals ($10bn+) projected **75–90 in 2026** (from 70).
- **Evercore (EVR):** now the **#3 franchise by advisory fees** (~$4.0bn trailing, behind only Goldman and JPMorgan, ahead of Morgan Stanley); **entered 2026 at an all-time-high backlog**; FY25 revenue **$3.88bn, +29%** (advisory +34%); record capital return.
- **Moelis (MC):** **record Q1 revenue $320m**, led by M&A and private-capital advisory.
- **Houlihan Lokey (HLI):** FY25 revenue **$2.39bn, +25%** — and structurally more stable (restructuring + mid-market blunt the cyclicality).
- **PJT Partners (PJT):** FY25 **$1.71bn, +15%**, advisory ~$1.5bn.

### Why the cycle has legs (management commentary + structural)
Record backlogs at Evercore and the bulge brackets mean **H1 2026 is largely pre-booked.** The fuel behind it is structural: **~$2.5tn of PE dry powder** and a multi-year backlog of un-exited PE portfolio companies that *have* to transact, plus a friendlier rate and regulatory backdrop. **73% of PE-backed sales >$500m** use an elite boutique or bulge bracket as lead — the boutiques are share-gainers within a growing pie.

### Why it's not fully priced
These are geared to a *recovery from depressed levels* — 2023–2024 advisory revenue was trough. The market still partly prices these as late-cycle/peak-earnings names and applies a cyclical discount, when backlogs and the PE-exit overhang argue the up-leg is early. Asset-light model = high incremental margins and most of earnings returned as dividends/buybacks while you wait.

### Risks / watch items
Genuinely cyclical — a financing-market shock or rates re-acceleration freezes deal flow fast; comp ratios can eat operating leverage in a talent war; and league-table position is competitive. This is a "buy the early up-cycle, don't overstay" theme — sized as a cyclical, not a compounder.

## Theme 3 — Copper / critical-metals miners: cheap, in structural deficit, and *not* a data-center trade *(deep-value cyclical)*

**The one-line thesis:** copper is heading into a **structural supply deficit** while trading at a single-digit cash-flow multiple. Crucially for this mandate, **data centers are only ~3% of global copper demand** even by 2030 — the demand is dominated by EVs, grid, and renewables. So you can own the electrification-metal deficit *without* it being an AI/data-center bet, and at a much cheaper multiple than the electrical-equipment names that already re-rated.

### The setup
- **Deficit is here:** the International Copper Study Group flipped its 2026 balance from a **+200kt surplus to a ~150kt deficit**; concentrate markets stay tight for years (cumulative ~3Mt deficit by 2036). New large-scale supply is scarce and slow.
- **Demand is electrification, not servers:** EVs use ~2.9x the copper of an ICE car; grid needs >$7.5tn of investment; clean-energy copper demand → ~61% of the transition by 2040. **Data centers ≈ 3% of global demand** — a marginal price-setter, not the driver. (This is the honest ex-data-center framing you asked for.)
- **Profitable and cheap:** **Freeport-McMoRan (FCX)** beat Q1 2026; FCF modeled to grow from **~$4.1bn (2026) to ~$7.7bn (2027)**; trades ~**8.1x NTM EV/EBITDA** — *below* Rio Tinto and Glencore's diversified multiples and well below Southern Copper's ~14.5x. Earnings scale with both volume growth *and* a rising deficit-driven price.

### Why it's not fully priced
Copper equities carry a "China-demand / global-cyclical" discount that predates the electrification thesis. The market is slow to capitalize a *structural* deficit into a *cyclical-miner* multiple — so you're paying a cyclical price for what is increasingly a secular-shortage asset.

### Risks / watch items
Most copper-price-sensitive of the three — earnings track the copper price, so a China hard-landing or global slowdown bites; project execution (Grasberg/Indonesia specifics for FCX); and tariff/policy noise around copper trade flows adds volatility. Own it as a cyclical with a structural tailwind, not a steady compounder.

**If I had to pick one:** **precious-metals miners.** It's the only one of the three where the operating leverage means the *earnings have already inflected violently* (Kinross margins +92%, FCF doubling), management is *proving* durability by paying it out (Agnico dividend +12.5%, Pan American targeting up to $1bn), and the equity has *demonstrably* lagged the underlying — the three-part test you set, all satisfied, in a corner of the market generalists still won't touch. Themes 2 and 3 are the diversifiers: one financials-cyclical (deal cycle), one materials-cyclical (copper deficit), neither dependent on the metal price, so together they don't all move on the same factor.

**Two honest caveats:** (1) all three are more *cyclical* than the earlier healthcare secular-growers — the trade-off for "profitable + cheap" is that you're taking commodity/deal-cycle beta, so these are sized and timed differently than a compounder. (2) I dropped cruise lines (RCL) despite a +33% EPS quarter because management *trimmed* full-year guidance ($17.70–18.10 → $17.10–17.50) — it fails your "management communicating positive uptake / guiding up" test, so it doesn't belong on the list.
