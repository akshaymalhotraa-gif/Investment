# Upcoming Growth Themes — Where Momentum Is Building but Valuation Hasn't Caught Up

**Compiled: 9 July 2026**

**Screen applied:** themes showing **1–3 quarters of strong, accelerating growth**, with **management explicitly guiding up / flagging positive uptake**, and where **valuation of at least one credible way-to-play has not fully discounted the runway.**

**Explicitly excluded (per mandate):** data-center / hyperscaler capex trades, semiconductors, and aerospace/defense. Where a theme brushes against the data-center trade (e.g. electrical equipment), I isolate the *non*-data-center driver and say so.

**How to read this:** each theme carries a **conviction tag**. Tier 1 = clean fit on all three tests (growth + guidance + valuation). Tier 2 = strong on growth/uptake but earlier-stage or with a valuation caveat you need to underwrite. The leaders in several of these themes are *not* cheap — the edge is usually in the second-tier or "pick-and-shovel" name, and I flag that explicitly.

## Theme 1 — Liquid-biopsy & MRD oncology diagnostics *(Tier 1)*

**The one-line thesis:** molecular residual disease (MRD) and blood-based cancer screening have flipped from "promising" to "reimbursed and scaling," and the whole cohort is posting 35–50% revenue growth with guidance raised — yet the category is still <15% penetrated into its addressable testing volume.

### The growth is real and accelerating (last 1–2 quarters)
- **Natera (NTRA):** Q1 2026 revenue **$696.6m, +38.8% YoY**; clinical MRD volumes ~**249k units, +55% YoY** — the fastest unit-growth quarter in its history, on a run-rate to **>1m MRD tests/year**. Raised FY26 guidance to **$2.74–2.82bn** (midpoint up ~$120m) and lifted gross-margin guidance to **~65%**. Signatera secured **full FDA approval in May 2026** — a genuine catalyst that widens reimbursement and pulls in community oncology.
- **Guardant Health (GH):** Q1 2026 revenue **+48% YoY**, driven by MRD and screening; raised FY26 guidance to **$1.30–1.32bn** (from $1.25–1.28bn). Owns **Shield**, the blood-based colorectal screen.
- **Tempus AI (TEM):** Q1 2026 revenue **$348.1m, +36.1% YoY**; **MRD volume +~500% YoY** off a small base; Data & Applications revenue **+40.5%**. Raised FY26 guidance to **$1.59–1.60bn**. The data-licensing leg is an under-appreciated, high-margin second engine.

### Management is leaning in
All three raised guidance in the same quarter and are pointing to volume — not price — as the driver, which is the healthy kind of beat. Natera's language ("fastest unit-growth quarter ever") and Tempus's 500% MRD ramp signal the category is at the steep part of the S-curve, not the top.

### The catalyst most people are underwriting too slowly
In **May 2026 the American Cancer Society added blood-based tests to its colorectal-screening guideline.** Guideline inclusion is the gate that converts a niche test into a standing-order screen. It's currently positioned as a second-line option (for people who decline colonoscopy/stool tests), so consensus is treating it cautiously — that's the mispricing: the *compliance* population (people who simply never get screened) is enormous and price-insensitive to sensitivity trade-offs.

### Valuation — where the edge actually is
- **Be honest about the leader:** Natera trades ~**14x forward sales** and is described by the sell-side as "stretched"; 3-yr forward revenue growth is modeled at ~15%, below the biotech-tools cohort. The market has *largely* priced Natera's MRD leadership. It's a quality compounder, not a value idea.
- **The value legs are (a) Guardant** — faster top-line growth (48% vs 39%) at a lower multiple, with both MRD *and* the Shield screening option; and **(b) Tempus** — the data-licensing business is being valued as a diagnostics lab when it's closer to a healthcare-data platform. That's the part "not fully priced."

### Watch items / risks
Reimbursement rate-setting (MolDx/ADLT pricing), sensitivity debates on blood-based screens vs colonoscopy, cash burn (Natera/Tempus still GAAP-unprofitable), and competitive MRD entry compressing per-test economics.

## Theme 2 — Metabolic-health delivery & the GLP-1 "pick-and-shovel" *(Tier 1 on the shovels, Tier 2 on delivery)*

**The one-line thesis:** the obesity *drug* trade (Lilly/Novo) is well-owned and fully narrated. The parts that are **still mispriced** are the *delivery* layer and the *supply chain* — oral GLP-1 launches in 2026 blow the addressable patient pool wide open, and the constraint shifts from "will it work" to "who can manufacture and distribute it."

### The demand inflection just happened (1–2 quarters)
- **Oral semaglutide (Wegovy pill)** launched in US pharmacies early January 2026; Novo reported **>600,000 prescriptions in March alone** — demand ahead of forecast.
- **Lilly's orforglipron (Foundayo)**, an oral small-molecule, followed in **April 2026** — mass-manufacturable in a way injectable peptides are not, which is the whole point for global scale.
- **CagriSema** FDA decision expected during 2026; ~28.7% weight loss at the top dose in a Phase 3 population.

### Way-to-play A — telehealth delivery: Hims & Hers (HIMS)
- **Raised FY26 guidance to $2.8–3.0bn (+19–28%)**; adjusted-EBITDA guide $275–350m; new **2030 targets of ≥$6.5bn revenue / $1.3bn EBITDA** (~20% CAGR).
- **The transition is the opportunity:** Hims wound down compounded GLP-1s and signed with **Novo Nordisk to distribute branded Wegovy/Ozempic** — this produced a **$92m Q1 loss** and margin compression, which spooked the market and compressed the multiple. If you believe the branded pivot is a durability upgrade (it is — removes the compounding legal overhang), the sell-off is the entry. **Tier 2 because:** execution/margin risk is real and the model is mid-transition.

### Way-to-play B — peptide CDMOs (the cleaner "shovel")
- North American peptide-CDMO capacity expansion is at a **five-year high**, with the market growing **12–16% CAGR through 2028**, driven by GLP-1 volume *plus* a US policy push to on-shore peptide API (today over-concentrated in APAC).
- This is the least-crowded way to own obesity: capacity is contracted years ahead, pricing is firm, and it's insulated from "which drug wins." Pure-play public exposure is thin and mostly European (Bachem, PolyPeptide) or embedded in larger names — which is *why* it's under-priced. Novo's $16.5bn Catalent purchase (+$11bn site transfer) shows what strategic buyers pay for this capacity.

### Watch items / risks
Payer coverage and net-pricing pressure as volume scales; muscle/lean-mass-loss becoming a clinical headwind (creates its own adjacency — resistance-training + protein + next-gen muscle-preserving agents); CDMO over-build if oral small-molecules (which need *chemical*, not peptide, synthesis) take share faster than expected — a genuine two-sided risk for the peptide-CDMO leg specifically.

## Theme 3 — Radioligand therapy (RLT) / radiopharmaceuticals *(Tier 2 — early innings, not yet priced)*

**The one-line thesis:** targeted radiation ("deliver the isotope to the tumor") is where antibody-drug-conjugates were ~5 years ago — proven biology (Novartis's Pluvicto), a scarce isotope/supply moat, and a market modeled to go from **~$3.2bn (2025) to ~$30bn (2034)**. The public pure-plays are small and, outside Novartis, largely un-owned by generalists.

### Evidence of uptake
- **Novartis Pluvicto** is scaling toward a stated **~$5bn peak**; label expansions add upside — this is the proof-of-commercial-model for the category.
- **Telix Pharmaceuticals (TLX)** is the credible listed pure-play with *actual revenue*: Q1 2026 Precision Medicine revenue **$186m, +22% YoY / +16% QoQ**, driven by Illuccix and the US launch of Gozellix; **reaffirmed FY26 guidance of $950–970m (~20%+ growth).** The market re-rated it +15% on the print — momentum is being recognized but the therapeutics pipeline is not yet in numbers.

### Why it's "not fully priced"
- **Lantheus (LNTH)** — the incumbent imaging-agent leader — is in a transition year (Q1 2026 revenue **+1.2%**; PYLARIFY **−6.5%** as it moves to the TruVu formulation), so the *stock* screens as un-loved even as it acquires its way into therapeutics (Life Molecular Imaging, Evergreen Theragnostics) and reaffirms **$1.4–1.45bn** revenue / **$5.00–5.25 EPS**. A flat-growth optical masking a therapeutics option is exactly the kind of setup that's under-discounted.
- The **alpha-emitter** sub-segment (actinium-225) is earlier still and modeled to grow into the multiple-billions — real option value in the clinical-stage names (Perspective Therapeutics, Clarity, Telix's therapeutic pipeline) but **underwrite these as biotech, not as compounders.**

### Watch items / risks
Isotope supply (Ac-225/Lu-177) is the true bottleneck and the moat — but also the risk; reimbursement and radiopharmacy logistics (short half-lives = distribution is a physical constraint); clinical readout binary risk in the pure-plays.

## Theme 4 — Identity security for the agentic-AI era (non-human / "machine" identity) *(Tier 2)*

**The one-line thesis:** every AI agent an enterprise deploys is a new identity that needs to be provisioned, governed and revoked. **Machine identities already outnumber human ones ~109:1**, and agents are the fastest-growing slice. This is a software theme with no data-center/semiconductor dependency — you're selling governance seats, not GPUs.

### The uptake signal
- **SailPoint (SAIL):** ARR **+26% YoY to ~$1.16bn**, **SaaS ARR +36%**, **raised full-year guidance**, and explicitly calling out **"Agentic" solutions** reshaping the revenue mix. This is the cleanest listed read on non-human identity governance.
- **Okta** launched its **"Blueprint for the Secure Agentic Enterprise"** and *Okta for AI Agents* (GA 30 April 2026) — productizing non-human identity visibility.
- **Validation via M&A:** **Palo Alto Networks is acquiring CyberArk** — the privileged-access leader (Q4'25 total ARR **$1.44bn, +23%**). A platform vendor paying up for identity-security is the strongest possible tell that this is a control-plane, not a feature.

### Why it's under-appreciated
Consensus still models identity vendors on *human* seat growth. The agent-identity TAM is additive and barely in estimates: **88% of organizations already report suspected/confirmed AI-agent security incidents, but only 22% treat agents as governed identities** — that gap is the growth runway. Gartner projects a quarter of enterprise breaches will involve AI-agent abuse by 2028.

### Watch items / risks
The CyberArk deal removes a standalone way-to-play (arbitrage only) and concentrates the theme; platform consolidation (Microsoft Entra bundling) is the perennial threat to identity point-solutions; "agentic" revenue is still small relative to the core seat business — don't over-pay on the narrative alone.

## Theme 5 — Electrification supply chain, *ex*-data-center: HVDC subsea cables & grid transformers *(Tier 2)*

**The one-line thesis:** the transformer and high-voltage-cable bottleneck is usually sold to investors as a *data-center* story. Strip that out and there's a **separate, multi-year, non-data-center demand wall**: offshore-wind interconnection, cross-border interconnectors, and the replacement of an aging grid. Lead times are measured in *years*, which means pricing power and backlog visibility the market underwrites conservatively.

### The non-data-center driver, isolated
- **Subsea HVDC cable order books have multi-year waitlists** — **DC cable lead times now exceed five years**, and only ~60 vessels worldwide can lay them. This demand is **offshore wind + interconnectors**, not server halls.
- **Prysmian:** order backlog **>€9bn**; committed **~$2bn capacity expansion through 2027**, doubling 525kV submarine and 400kV AC cable output. Targeting €140–160m run-rate synergies.
- **Transformers:** a structural shortage (Hitachi Energy alone committing ~$1bn to a new Virginia transformer plant) driven by grid replacement and electrification of industry — a slow-moving, supply-constrained cycle.
- **Quanta Services (PWR):** record **Electric Infrastructure RPO / total backlog ~$44bn** at YE2025 — the labor/EPC bottleneck for actually building the grid.

### Why the "ex-data-center" framing matters for valuation
Names like Eaton/nVent get bid *and re-rated* on data-center order acceleration (Eaton's data-center orders were +240% and it raised FY26 organic growth to 10% from 8%). Because they're tagged as "AI trades," they're not cheap. **The cable/transformer/EPC layer is valued more like slow industrials** even though its backlog is longer-dated and *less* cyclical — that's the inefficiency. You're buying the part of electrification that survives even if AI capex disappoints.

### Watch items / risks
This is the softest fit on the "clean growth acceleration" test — it's a backlog/visibility story more than a just-inflected one; offshore-wind project cancellations (permitting, rates) are the key demand risk; and honestly, some of these order books *do* include data-center load, so diligence each name's mix rather than taking the theme at face value.

**If I had to pick two to start:** (1) the **Guardant/Tempus** leg of liquid-biopsy — clearest growth-plus-catalyst with a defensible "the leader is priced, these aren't" argument; and (2) **peptide CDMOs** as the least-crowded obesity exposure. Both let you own a proven, accelerating secular without paying the multiple that the obvious leader (Natera, Lilly/Novo) already commands.

**Cross-cutting caveat:** three of these five (1, 2-delivery, 3) sit in unprofitable or transitioning healthcare names where a risk-off tape hits multiples hard regardless of fundamentals — size accordingly, and prefer the profitable/backlog-anchored legs (Guardant's path to profitability, peptide CDMOs, Quanta/Prysmian) if you want the theme with less drawdown beta.
