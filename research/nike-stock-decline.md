# NIKE, Inc. (NYSE: NKE) — Why the Stock Has Fallen So Sharply: A Historical Post-Mortem

**Compiled: 13 July 2026** | Fiscal year ends 31 May
**Scope:** the ~75–77% drawdown from the all-time high of **$179.10 (5 November 2021)** to roughly **$40 (July 2026)** — four consecutive down years, over a period in which the S&P 500 gained ~60%. Peak market cap was ~$280bn; today it is in the ~$60bn range.

---

## 1. The setup: how Nike got to $179 (context for the fall)

The decline can't be understood without the run-up. Between 2017 and late 2021 Nike roughly tripled:

- **Pandemic winner.** Lockdowns super-charged athleisure demand and e-commerce; Nike's digital business grew at rates the market extrapolated indefinitely.
- **The DTC narrative.** In June 2020 CEO **John Donahoe** (ex-eBay/ServiceNow, appointed Jan 2020) launched the **Consumer Direct Acceleration (CDA)** strategy: exit hundreds of wholesale accounts (Foot Locker, DSW, Zappos, regional retailers) and push consumers to Nike's own stores, apps (Nike App, SNKRS) and website. Wall Street loved it — direct sales carry higher gross margin, and Nike was re-rated from a retailer multiple toward a "tech-enabled brand platform" multiple.
- **Peak valuation.** At the November 2021 top, Nike traded at roughly **45x+ forward earnings** — pricing in permanent double-digit growth, permanent DTC margin expansion, and continued dominance in China.

Almost every leg of that thesis subsequently broke. The stock fall is the compounding of **five distinct shocks** plus a **valuation reset**, described chronologically below.

---

## 2. Phase 1 (2021–22): China breaks and supply chains seize

- **Xinjiang cotton boycott (March 2021).** After Nike (and H&M, adidas) stated they would not source Xinjiang cotton, a nationalist social-media backlash in China became a durable inflection point. Domestic brands **Anta and Li-Ning** took share permanently: by January 2022 they held ~28% of Chinese sneaker sales (up 12pp since the boycott), while foreign brands' sales fell ~24%. Greater China — Nike's highest-margin, fastest-growing geography and the core of the bull case — never recovered its trajectory.
- **Vietnam COVID shutdowns (H2 2021).** Nike sources roughly half of its footwear from Vietnam; months-long factory closures cost tens of millions of units and scrambled order books — the seed of the next crisis.
- **Macro de-rating (2022).** Rising rates compressed all long-duration growth multiples. Nike, at 45x earnings, had much further to fall than the market. A meaningful share of the total drawdown is simply multiple compression: from ~45x to the low/mid-20s even before earnings deteriorated.

## 3. Phase 2 (2022–23): the inventory glut and margin crash

- Post-shutdown, the delayed Vietnam supply arrived **just as demand normalised**. In September 2022 Nike reported North America inventories up ~65% (global +44%); the stock fell ~13% in a day.
- Clearing the glut required heavy **markdowns and off-price dumping**, compressing gross margin by ~300bps and — more damagingly — eroding the **full-price, scarcity-driven brand positioning** Nike had spent years building (SNKRS drops, limited editions).
- China compounded it: zero-COVID lockdowns, plus the continuing consumer shift to cheaper local brands.

## 4. Phase 3 (2023–24): the strategy itself fails — CDA unwinds

This is the heart of the story; the earlier shocks were cyclical, this one was self-inflicted and structural:

- **Wholesale exits backfired.** Cutting off Foot Locker, JD Sports and others didn't convert those sales to nike.com — it handed shelf space to competitors. Retailers, burned by Nike, diversified their walls toward **Hoka (Deckers), On Running, New Balance, Asics** — exactly as those brands hit their innovation stride in running.
- **Innovation drought / franchise fatigue.** Under CDA, Nike had reorganised away from sport-specific teams toward gender/digital categories, and monetised demand by flooding the market with **retro lifestyle franchises — Air Force 1, Dunk, Jordan 1**. Margins looked great until the franchises saturated and cooled; meanwhile the performance-running pipeline thinned just as On and Hoka redefined the category. Nike lost serious share among runners — historically its brand heartland.
- **December 2023:** Nike announced a **$2bn three-year cost-cutting plan** and cut revenue guidance; shares fell ~12%.
- **28 June 2024 — the record one-day crash.** With Q4 FY24 results Nike guided to a ~10% revenue decline for the next quarter (vs ~3% expected) and flagged FY25 sales down mid-single digits. The stock fell **~20%, erasing $28.4bn of market value — the worst day since the 1980 IPO** — and at least six banks downgraded. Pressure on Donahoe became public and intense.
- **September–October 2024:** Donahoe out; company veteran **Elliott Hill** (32 years at Nike, ran commercial/marketplace operations) returned as CEO. The stock jumped ~7–8% on the announcement, but the new team promptly **withdrew full-year guidance** — resetting expectations for a long repair job.

## 5. Phase 4 (2025–26): turnaround pain + tariffs

Hill's **"Win Now"** plan is deliberately deflationary in the short run — shrink the over-distributed classics (Air Force 1, Dunk, AJ1), clean up the marketplace, rebuild wholesale trust, and re-centre the company on sport (the **"sport offense"** realignment announced with FY26 results). That means several quarters of engineered revenue decline before growth can resume:

- **FY2025 (ended May 2025): revenue ~$46bn, down ~10%** from FY24's ~$51bn peak, with Nike Digital falling sharply as paid traffic and franchise volumes were intentionally cut.
- **Tariff shock (April 2025).** The US "reciprocal" tariff regime hit Vietnam-/Indonesia-/China-centric sourcing hard; Nike flagged a **~$1.5bn annualised tariff headwind**, and the stock made multi-year lows in the low-$50s. (Partial reprieve: after the Supreme Court struck down many IEEPA duties, Nike's Q4 FY26 included an expected **~$986m tariff refund**, worth $0.52 of the $0.72 reported EPS.)
- **FY2026 (ended May 2026):** roughly flat revenue (~$46bn) with the mix shifting as intended — Q2 FY26 wholesale **+8%** while Nike Direct fell **8%**; full-year Nike Digital **-12%**, Nike-owned stores **-4%**. But the problem areas remain stark: **Greater China down ~17–20%** in Q4 with worse guided, **Converse down ~31%** for the year, and gross margin at **~40%** versus On at ~64% and Lululemon at ~55%. Q4 (reported 30 June 2026) beat expectations ex-tariff-refund, but management guided to **"flattish" earnings and further H1 FY27 revenue declines**, and the stock still fell — closing in on ~$40, i.e. ~77% off the 2021 peak. Citi cut its target to $45; Barclays to $52 (Overweight).

## 6. The causes, ranked (structural view)

| # | Cause | Nature | Status (Jul 2026) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **CDA/DTC strategy error** — wholesale exits, retro over-distribution, innovation drought | Self-inflicted, structural | Being unwound under Hill; wholesale recovering, Digital deliberately shrinking |
| 2 | **China deterioration** — Xinjiang boycott, nationalism, Anta/Li-Ning share gains, weak consumer | External + structural | Still declining (Q4 FY26 China -17% cc, -20% guided) |
| 3 | **Competitive fragmentation** — Hoka, On, New Balance, Asics in running; premium yoga/athleisure to Lululemon/Alo/Vuori | Structural | Ongoing; Nike rebuilding performance pipeline (Pegasus/Vomero/Structure relaunches) |
| 4 | **Valuation reset** — 45x+ peak multiple in a zero-rate world | Cyclical | Done; the multiple is no longer the problem, earnings are |
| 5 | **Inventory glut & markdowns (2022–23)** — brand-diluting discounting | Cyclical, mostly resolved | Resolved, but full-price positioning damaged |
| 6 | **Tariffs & sourcing concentration (2025–26)** — ~$1.5bn headwind, partially refunded | External, policy-dependent | ~$986m IEEPA recovery booked Q4 FY26; residual uncertainty |
| 7 | **Leadership churn / execution** — Donahoe era, guidance withdrawal, $2bn restructuring | Governance | Hill installed Oct 2024; "sport offense" org now in place |

**One-line synthesis:** Nike is not a company that had one bad year — it stacked a strategic error (abandoning wholesale and living off retro franchises) on top of losing its best growth market (China) at the exact moment credible competitors emerged in its core category (running), all starting from the most expensive valuation in its history; the 2025–26 tariff regime and the deliberate pain of the turnaround then extended the drawdown into a fourth and fifth year.

## 7. Watch items for a recovery thesis

- **Wholesale sell-through** of the rebuilt performance running line (Pegasus/Vomero families) — the cleanest read on whether brand heat is returning.
- **Greater China stabilisation** — guidance still points down ~20%; any inflection is a major swing factor.
- **Nike Direct finding its floor** once franchise cuts anniversary (Digital was -12% in FY26 by design).
- **Gross margin path back toward the mid-40s** ex-tariff noise; the ~40% print vs On/Lulu shows how much full-price recovery is left.
- **FY27 guidance posture** — management has signalled flattish earnings and H1 revenue declines; the first quarter of genuine growth guidance would mark the turn.
- **Jordan/sportswear streetwear demand** — flagged by analysts as still deteriorating even as performance stabilises.
