## Theme 1 — Integrated payments + vertical software: the "experience economy" fintech *(highest conviction this round)*

**The one-line thesis:** software that runs a specific industry (restaurants, hotels, stadiums, retail) with payments bolted directly in monetizes every transaction *and* the software seat. The category is compounding 25–35% with expanding margins and real free cash flow — but it's valued like a "payments processor" (commodity, low multiple) rather than the vertical-software-plus-take-rate machine it actually is. Zero data-center/semiconductor dependency; you're selling to Main Street.

### The numbers already printed (Q1 2026)
- **Shift4 (FOUR):** gross revenue **+32% to $1.12bn**; **gross revenue *less network fees* +49%** (the number that matters); **adjusted EBITDA +39%; FCF +26%.** Reaffirmed FY26 guidance of **$2.5–2.6bn** revenue-less-network-fees (**+26–31%**). Expanding from restaurants into hotels, sports/entertainment, and international (Global Blue).
- **Toast (TOST):** profitable, high-20s%+ growth in a huge under-penetrated restaurant TAM; ~**26x forward earnings** — reasonable for the growth/FCF profile.

### Management is leaning in
Shift4 reaffirmed guidance *through* a "challenging macro" and Middle-East-conflict headwind — the tell that the model's land-and-cross-sell engine is compounding independent of the cycle. The strategic language is all about widening the moat in the "experience economy" verticals where generic processors can't follow.

### Why it's not fully priced
The market anchors payments names to interchange/processing comps and slaps a discount on integration risk (Shift4's acquisitiveness in particular). But the economics are software-like: high incremental margins, sticky vertical SaaS, and a take-rate that grows with every new merchant vertical. **FOUR specifically trades at a free-cash-flow discount** because of the integration overhang — that's the mispricing if you think management executes the Global Blue/international build-out.

### Risks / watch items
Integration/M&A risk is the real one (Shift4 is a serial acquirer — execution and leverage matter); consumer-spend sensitivity (restaurants/hotels are discretionary); and competition from the broader processor field. Own FOUR as the higher-growth/higher-risk name, TOST as the cleaner single-vertical compounder.

## Theme 2 — Prediction markets + iGaming: the regulated-betting TAM expansion *(genuinely "upcoming," profitable core)*

**The one-line thesis:** the *new* leg here isn't sports betting — it's **CFTC-regulated prediction markets**, which create a **federal** path to take event-contract "bets" in the big states that have *rejected* sports-betting legislation (California, Texas, Georgia). That's a step-change in addressable market layered on top of an already-profitable online-gambling core, and it's happening *now* (volumes tripling month-on-month). The incumbents are profitable; the option value on prediction markets is barely in estimates.

### The numbers / the inflection (Q1 2026)
- **DraftKings (DKNG):** sportsbook revenue **+24% to $1.09bn**; iGaming **$461m (+9%)** and described as *profitable and under-penetrated*; company is GAAP-profitable. **Prediction-market annualized consumer volume passed $1bn in April** (total annualized volume **$2.3bn, +43% MoM**) — a faster ramp than iGaming itself had. Sell-side is beginning to model prediction markets as **10–15% of 2027 EBITDA.**
- **The structural unlock:** the CFTC-supervised event-contract structure lets operators offer sports contracts in states that banned traditional sportsbooks — i.e., it routes *around* the state-by-state legalization bottleneck that has capped this sector for years.
- **Additional catalysts:** iGaming (online casino) state legalization pending in **Virginia, New York** and others — iGaming is the higher-margin, faster-growing product and is *outgrowing* sports betting in North America.

### Why it's not fully priced
The market is discounting the *near-term cost* of standing up prediction markets (DraftKings guided a **$200–300m 2026 EBITDA drag** for the investment) and largely ignoring the *TAM it opens*. Classic "penalized for the investment, not credited for the option." The less-obvious way to play the iGaming leg is a pure-play like **Rush Street Interactive (RSI)** — iGaming-centric operators have been *outperforming* the sportsbook-heavy names.

### Risks / watch items — read these before acting
This is the most caveated theme in the note. **Flutter/FanDuel *cut* full-year guidance on U.S. margin pressure**, and prediction markets are cannibalizing some sportsbook revenue and inviting new competition (Kalshi et al.). Regulatory risk cuts both ways — the CFTC pathway could tighten. Near-term EBITDA is being *spent*, not harvested. Treat this as "own the profitable core + a cheap call option on prediction markets," sized for volatility — not a clean compounder.

## Theme 3 — Water infrastructure & "smart water": the durable, un-sexy compounder *(defensive leg)*

**The one-line thesis:** replacing a century-old, leaking U.S. water grid is a multi-decade, recession-resistant capital cycle funded by municipalities and federal infrastructure money. The distribution and products layer is profitable, steadily growing, raising dividends — and the *newer* leg (smart metering / leak-detection analytics) is inflecting into double digits. It's the theme you own for ballast when the cyclicals above wobble.

### The numbers (last 1–2 quarters)
- **Core & Main (CNM):** fiscal-Q1 net sales **$1.91bn**; reaffirmed FY26 **$7.8–7.9bn**; municipal repair-and-replace demand "healthy," **treatment-plant solutions growing double digits**, smart-utility products high-single-digit. The distribution consolidator of a fragmented market.
- **Advanced Drainage Systems (WMS):** FY26 sales **$3.05bn (+5%)**, **wastewater +13%**; Q4 sales **+9.9%**; FY27 guide stepped up to **$3.35–3.55bn**; **dividend raised 13%.**
- **Mueller Water Products (MWA):** Water Management Solutions **+12.2%**; growth in the **Sentryx** water-intelligence / **EchoShore** leak-detection platforms — the higher-margin analytics inflection inside a legacy hardware business.

### Why it's here (and an honest caveat)
This is the *steadiest* theme, not the fastest — headline growth is mid-single to low-double digits, so it fails a strict "explosive growth" test. What earns its place: **rising dividends + guidance step-ups + a genuine inflection in the smart-water/analytics leg**, all funded by non-discretionary municipal budgets. Valuation is reasonable on the distribution names (CNM), richer on ADS. Note the *one* overlap risk with your exclusions: data-center water cooling is now a marginal demand source these names get tagged with — but the core driver is municipal repair-and-replace, not server halls.

### Risks / watch items
Municipal budget timing and interest-rate sensitivity of public-works funding; input-cost (resin/steel) swings for the products names; and the theme's virtue (stability) is also its ceiling — don't expect a cyclical's upside.
